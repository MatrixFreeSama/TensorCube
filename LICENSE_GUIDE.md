# MFSPL 2.0 practical guide

This guide is explanatory only. If it conflicts with `LICENSE`, the license text controls.

## Default rule

MFSPL 2.0 is intentionally very permissive before a direct Provenance Notice is received.

A recipient may copy, modify, port, refactor, close-source, commercialize, rename, redistribute, sublicense, and incorporate Covered Material into another project without a default duty to name MatrixFreeSama, Zining Kestis Wong, TensorCube, or any contributor. The recipient is not required by the license to retain the license text or a copyright notice in a redistribution.

The license does not create immunity from generally applicable law. It does not authorize fraud, trademark infringement, impersonation, false advertising, or other independently unlawful conduct.

## What the dormant provenance right does

Only a person or entity that is legally bound by MFSPL 2.0 and actually receives a valid direct Provenance Notice can acquire the Section 4 obligations.

The notice can require:

- a factual acknowledgement that the identified implementation or material derives from MatrixFreeSama-distributed research/software/material;
- a truthful answer when the Licensor directly asks about that derivation;
- a specified public-facing `Designated Project Credit`;
- removal or replacement of the recipient's project-level public credit where the notice says so.

The normal compliance period is 30 days after actual receipt.

## Project credit is not legal authorship

`Designated Project Credit` is deliberately separate from legal authorship.

The Licensor may designate a real person, organization, pseudonym, fictional character, fictional entity, non-participant, `Anonymous`, or other text as the public-facing project credit. Doing so does not make that identity the legal author, owner, contributor, or endorser.

This distinction is intentional. The license controls project presentation only to the extent permitted by law; it does not rewrite historical fact.

## Independent ideas and independent implementations are free

MFSPL 2.0 does not attempt to control similarity of ideas.

A person who merely reads a paper, learns an algorithmic idea, is inspired by the research direction, independently rediscovers the same mathematics, or independently writes a new implementation without copying or adapting protected expression does not become bound merely because the resulting idea or algorithm is highly similar.

## Earlier MFSPL 1.0 copies

MFSPL 2.0 is prospective. It does not retroactively impose its Provenance Notice covenant on copies that were already licensed solely under MFSPL 1.0.

An earlier recipient can come under MFSPL 2.0 only if MFSPL 2.0 is offered for that copy and the recipient accepts that offer to the extent required by applicable law.
